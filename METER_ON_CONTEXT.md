## 6. The concept: Meter On

- **Logline:** Book an auto. Your driver is a 21-year-old founder. The meter runs on words.
- **Theme: nothing moves until you speak.**
  - The auto runs on voice: it moves while I talk and idles when I go quiet.
  - The jam is silence: when the talking stops, everything stops.
  - The reroute begins when I speak again.
  - The ride cannot end until the visitor speaks too.
- **Roles:** the visitor is the passenger · Sky is the driver (animated, stylized) · the dashboard phone is the co-pilot that turns messy speech into clean text · the auto is a character.
- **Camera: a mix.** Cinematic shots (wide, front three-quarter, drone, macro) for big story moments; inside POV for talking moments and interactions.
- **Core mechanics:**
  1. **The word meter:** ticks once per spoken word.
  2. **Messy to clean:** each line appears raw, the fillers strike out and fade, the clean line lifts onto the windshield.
  3. **The road is memory:** the world outside transforms with the story.
  4. **Dusk to sunrise:** the light carries the emotional arc.
- **Payoffs:**
  - **The trip log:** every clean line from the ride, folded into one page titled "The resume I never had to type".
  - **The fare:** the visitor speaks for 30 seconds and gets a shareable ride receipt. This is the growth loop.
- **Why it fits this team:** voice is the engine; it is unmistakably Bengaluru; it nods to their auto launch; it shows growth thinking instead of claiming it.

## 9. Website flow (v1)

1. **Booking screen:** night-mode illustrated map (not a real map provider), a small auto moving toward the pickup pin, the booking card, the driver card (Sky, new driver, KA 05 SKY 21), address chips, "This is a voice story. Sound on.", and a **Book ride** button. Scene 1 preloads here.
2. **Countdown:** "Driver arriving 3, 2, 1", then dissolve into Scene 1.
3. **The ride:** route bar on top (11 stops, scrubbable), meter at top right, captions at the bottom, and controls (pause, previous and next stop, mute, 1x or 1.25x; keyboard: Space, arrows, M).
4. **Hotspots:** glow when tappable; a tap pauses and opens a card; closing resumes.
5. **Holds:** the horn (up to 8 s) and the reroute choice (up to 3 s). Neither can block the ride.
6. **Arrival:** the growth card expands into the "If I joined Wispr Flow India" page.
7. **The fare:** the mic is requested only here. 30-second ring timer, live raw-to-clean transcript, meter counts the visitor's words, typed fallback, and the privacy line "Your voice stays in your browser. Nothing is recorded or stored."
8. **Receipt:** a vertical story-sized card with the route doodle, how they were addressed, word count, ums removed, their best clean line, stickers collected, "Rode with Sky · Meter On". Download, copy link, share. A shared link opens with their receipt first: "Your friend paid this fare. Book your own ride."
9. **Trip log:** every clean line with proof links, a "Made by voice" panel, **Rate your driver** (Hire / Strongly hire), Ride again, and a post-credit shot of the auto driving off with "Who's next?".

Later (v1.1+): Quick ride (60 s), mirror questions, optional voice controls during the ride, director's cut video.

## 12. Tech architecture (summary; full detail in METER_ON_V1_PROMPT.md)

- Vite + React + TypeScript; three.js via React Three Fiber, drei and postprocessing; GSAP; Zustand; Web Speech API; qrcode; html-to-image; deployed on Vercel.
- **Everything is a pure function of ride time `t`** plus the visitor's choices. One clock drives the camera, scenes, meter, captions and audio, which makes pause, seek, deep links (`?stop=jam`) and a later Remotion director's cut straightforward.
- Content lives in `script.json` (scenes, lines, words, shots, hotspots, holds) and `config.ts`. Directing happens in `shots.ts`.
- v1 uses procedural, stylized assets with a swap layer for real 3D models and audio later.
